Is the house in Tacoma?

Why this matters: cities can have their own landlord rules on top of the state’s. Tacoma has strict ones. So the first question is whose rules this house falls under.

Confirmed Sep 14, 2026

The house is in unincorporated Pierce County, not the City of Tacoma. Tacoma’s rental rules do not apply here. Washington state law does.

Unincorporated means the area is not part of any city: no city government and no city code. Pierce County runs it, and the state’s landlord law applies directly. The Tacoma mailing address comes from the Post Office. It says nothing about whose rules apply.

If Tacoma ever annexes the area, Tacoma’s rules would start to apply. Sources: WA DOR location code 2727, Census geocoder, Pierce County parcel search.

Do you need a business license?

A business license is a registration with the city or state that says you are running a business. Some places require one to rent out a house. Randy heard that Tacoma will not let a landlord evict without one.

The answer

No business license is needed for this house, the way it is run today.

Why not
  • The rule you heard is Tacoma’s. Inside the city, every rental needs a city business license, and without one a landlord cannot serve a notice to vacate, raise the rent or evict. It stops at the city limits.
  • Pierce County has no general business license and no rental registration.
  • Washington requires a state business license once a business takes in $12,000 a year of income the state taxes. Long-term rent (30 days or more) is exempt from that tax, so one house rented in Mikaela’s own name does not trigger it.
What would change that
  • Putting the house into an LLC. The LLC needs a state license.
  • Renting under a business name instead of Mikaela’s legal name.
  • Hiring someone as an employee.
  • Renting it short-term, under 30 days at a time.
  • Tacoma annexing the area. Then the city license comes before any notice.
Getting one anyway
  • For: a state business number and a registered business name, handy for a separate bank account and a business name on the lease.
  • For: already in place if the house ever moves into an LLC.
  • Against: it gives no legal protection. Protection comes from landlord insurance, and from an LLC if one is ever formed.
  • Against: once registered, the state expects tax returns on a schedule, even at zero, unless she asks for a no-report status, plus yearly renewals.
  • Bottom line: not worth it for one house in her name. A separate bank account for the house keeps the books just as clean without the paperwork.

Sources: Tacoma rental license, Tacoma code 1.95, Pierce County code 5.12, WAC 458-20-101, WAC 458-20-118.

Washington landlord law

Washington has one law that covers renting a home, called the Residential Landlord-Tenant Act. These are the parts that apply to this house, and what each one means in practice. Current as of September 2026.

Ending a tenancy takes a reason

Washington calls this just cause. A tenancy can only be ended for a reason on the state’s list, with the notice that reason requires.

  • The exception: a first lease of 12 months or more can simply end on its end date, with 60 days’ written notice.
  • Unpaid rent: 14 days’ notice to pay or leave.
  • Breaking the lease: 10 days’ notice to fix it.
  • Damage, nuisance or illegal activity: 3 days.
  • Mikaela or close family moving in, or selling the house: 90 days.
  • Every notice has to state the specific facts. Ending a tenancy without a valid reason can cost three months’ rent plus the tenant’s legal fees.
Rent increases are capped

A 2025 state law limits rent increases on this house. Many landlord websites say single-family homes owned by individuals are exempt. The law itself only exempts homes the owner lives in, so this house is covered.

  • No increase at all in the first 12 months of a tenancy.
  • After that, at most 7% plus inflation per 12 months, never more than 10%. The state set the cap at 9.683% for 2026 and 10% for 2027.
  • 90 days’ written notice, on the state’s form, and no increase during a fixed-term lease.
  • Example: at $2,400 a month, the most a 2027 increase can add is $240, to $2,640.
  • What it means now: set the starting rent carefully. It cannot be corrected for a year, and after that only within the cap.
If rent goes unpaid

The first step is a 14-day pay-or-vacate notice.

  • It has to follow the state’s wording, including the legal aid contacts. A notice with the wrong wording, or served the wrong way, has to be redone.
  • After posting it on the door, a copy goes by regular mail.
  • Tenants who cannot afford a lawyer get one free from the court, so expect weeks, not days.
The deposit
  • It can only be taken with a written lease and a move-in condition checklist signed and dated by both sides. Without the checklist, none of it can be kept.
  • It goes into a trust account, with a written receipt naming the bank.
  • Return it, or send an itemized list of deductions, within 30 days after move-out.
  • The tenant can ask in writing to pay the deposit and fees in three monthly installments. That can only be refused if the total is 25% of a month’s rent or less and last month’s rent is not required up front.
  • A fee called nonrefundable has to say so in the written lease. Otherwise it counts as refundable.
Screening applicants
  • Disclose the criteria, the screening service and the fee before running a check. The fee can only cover the actual cost.
  • Give a written reason if someone is declined.
  • All lawful income counts, housing vouchers included. Families with children and assistance animals cannot be screened out.
Entering the house
  • Two days’ written notice to enter while a tenant lives there.
  • One day’s notice to show it to a future tenant or buyer.
  • An emergency does not need notice.
Safety
  • Working smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms are the landlord’s duty, and so is a livable house: heat, water, a sound roof, working plumbing and electrical.
  • 1961 build: lead paint rules apply to the disclosure and to any work that disturbs old paint.
Chelsea and David
  • If they ever paid a deposit, it is due back with an itemized statement within 30 days after they leave.

Sources: RCW 59.18.650, 59.18.710, Commerce rent cap, 59.18.057, 59.18.260, 59.18.610, 59.18.285, 59.18.257, 59.18.150, 59.18.060.

Taxes

How a rental house is taxed, in plain terms. This is research, not tax advice: the person who does Mikaela’s taxes confirms each point before anything is filed or timed. Many tax preparers are CPAs, certified public accountants; either can handle this.

How the IRS sees the house today

When a house is rented to family for less than market rent, the IRS treats it as a personal home, not a rental business. That is how the years with Chelsea and David count.

  • Rental expenses and depreciation were likely not deductible in those years. Property tax and mortgage interest stay deductible the usual way, on the personal side.
  • The rent received was still income. Worth checking that her past returns reported it.
  • 2026 counts the same way, because family lived there until October.
When to pay for the work

The year a bill is paid decides which tax year it lands in, and 2026 and 2027 are treated very differently.

  • Money spent October to December 2026 is mostly not deductible, because 2026 is still a family year.
  • The same bills paid in January 2027 land in the first real rental year.
  • So: safety and must-do work now, and the washer, dryer and anything that can wait in January, if the tax person agrees.
What a rental can deduct, starting 2027

A deduction is an expense subtracted from income before tax is figured. Once the house is a real rental, these count.

  • Mortgage interest, property tax, landlord insurance, property manager fees, repairs, cleaning, inspections, and mileage to the house (with a log).
  • Depreciation: the building, not the land, is written off over 27.5 years. The split between land and building comes from the county assessor’s values in the year she bought it.
  • The washer and dryer can be written off in full the year they go in: items under $2,500 each can be expensed, and the 2025 tax law made 100% first-year write-offs permanent.
Repairs and improvements are taxed differently

A repair keeps the house as it was and is deducted the same year. An improvement makes it better, replaces a major part, or changes how a space is used, and is written off a little each year instead.

  • Repairs: paint, patching floors, fixing what is broken.
  • Improvements: a new roof, replacing all the flooring, getting the garage room permitted.
  • A small-landlord rule lets roughly the first $5,000 of work in a year be deducted outright on this house (the lesser of $10,000 or 2% of the building’s value).
If the house shows a loss on paper

Once depreciation is counted, a rental often shows a loss at tax time even when the rent covers the mortgage. That paper loss can lower the tax on other income, within limits.

  • Up to $25,000 a year of rental losses can offset other income when income is under $100,000. The allowance shrinks to zero at $150,000.
  • Her income drops in 2027, which may put her under that line. How she files matters: single, married jointly, or married separately. Married filing separately while living together gets nothing.
  • She has to make the big calls herself (rent, tenant, spending) to qualify, even with a property manager. Keep the emails that show it.
Washington state taxes
  • No state income tax on the rent.
  • No state business tax on long-term rent, and no city tax in unincorporated Pierce County.
  • Property tax continues as usual.
If the house is ever sold
  • No home-sale tax break, because she never lived there.
  • Depreciation taken, or that could have been taken, is taxed at up to 25% on a sale.
  • A 1031 exchange, rolling the proceeds into another rental, defers all of it.
  • Washington’s capital gains tax does not apply to real estate. The state’s sale tax, about 1.6% here, is usually paid by the seller.
